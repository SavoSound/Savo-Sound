3 D

3 D is a room reverb modeled from the natural ambience captured in a real vocal recording.

The reference was recorded in a small living room with acoustic treatment positioned around the vocalist and microphone. The resulting ambience was short and controlled, sitting naturally behind the direct vocal rather than competing with it. That captured room character became the foundation for 3 D.

Rather than starting with a conventional room algorithm and tuning it by ear, several phrase endings in the recording were analyzed to examine how the captured room energy developed over time and across the frequency spectrum. Measurements from four vocal stops were combined to reduce the influence of individual notes and phrases. The resulting energy profile was used to shape 36 early reflections, while differences between frequency bands helped define the tonal character and decay of the room. A network of 16 delay lines was then used to construct the diffuse tail.

Because the original recording was effectively mono, it contained no reliable information about the physical direction of the reflections. The stereo image was therefore built around the measured character of the mono recording rather than an assumed reconstruction of the room's geometry. 3 D is an algorithmic room model based on characteristics measured from the recording — not an extracted impulse response.

At ORIGINAL, the model preserves the compact reflections, decay behaviour and tonal character derived from the recording. Turning up ROOM expands the acoustic scale while retaining those characteristics, progressively transforming the original small room into increasingly larger versions of itself rather than morphing into a different reverb.

The recording that started it all happened to be made in apartment 3D — hence the name.

At the opposite end of the ROOM control is BJØNNDIGERT — a Norwegian term that, in this context, can reasonably be translated as fucking huge.

One room. From ORIGINAL to BJØNNDIGERT.
